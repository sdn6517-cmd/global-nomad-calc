What actually decides your AI bill

Two teams can send the same 10,000 prompts a day and pay a bill a hundred times apart without either of them being wrong. The difference is never the "best model" headline — it is five levers, in this order of impact.

1. Output tokens cost five to ten times input tokens

Every vendor prices generated tokens far above read tokens, because generation is the expensive part of inference. On GPT-6 Astra one million output tokens cost $50 against $10 for input; on Sonnet 5.5 it is $10 against $2. That asymmetry means the cheapest optimisation is almost always shorter answers: a 200-token cap instead of 800 cuts the bigger half of the bill by 75%, and users rarely notice.

2. A cached input token is billed at about a tenth

Prompt caching stores the prefix of a request — a long system prompt, a policy document, a few hundred rows of context — and charges a cache-read price on the next call. On Sonnet 5.5 that is $0.10 instead of $2.00 per million tokens. If 80% of a 1,500-token prompt is a stable prefix, moving the cache slider on this page from 0% to 80% typically removes two-thirds of the input side of the bill. The catch: a cache write costs 1.25× a normal input token, so caching only pays when the same prefix is reused, not once.

3. Reasoning tokens are output tokens

A reasoning model that "thinks" for 3,000 tokens before a 300-token answer is billed for 3,300 output tokens. This is why a model with a mid-range price can lose to a cheaper one on real traffic, and why effort settings exist: dropping from high to medium reasoning effort often halves the bill with no visible change in answer quality for extraction and routing tasks.

4. Context length can trigger a different price list

Several models change price at a context threshold rather than charging linearly: GPT-6 Astra bills 2× input and 1.5× output for requests above 272K tokens; Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.1 Pro step up above 200K; Claude Haiku 5.5 jumps from $0.10 to $0.50 per million input tokens once a prompt passes 100K. Pasting a whole PDF into a prompt is exactly the move that trips these thresholds.

5. Traffic shape decides whether batch applies

Anything that does not need an answer in the next minute — nightly enrichment, backfills, evaluation runs, tagging — can usually go through a batch or flex tier at 50% off. For a team whose prompts are half interactive and half background, splitting the two workloads is a straight 25% cut with no product change.

A worked example

A product with 1,000 active users sends 90,000 prompts a month — three per user a day, 1,500 input and 500 output tokens each. That is 135 million input tokens and 45 million output tokens every month. On GPT-6 Astra at list price the bill is $3,600 a month. The same workload on Claude Sonnet 5.5 is $720, and on Gemini 3.8 Flash at its current introductory rate it is $270. Identical traffic, 13× difference. None of these numbers says which model is good enough for your task — but seeing the spread is what turns the choice from an accident into a decision.

Why "I want $90k, so I charge $45/hour" fails

Dividing a target by 2,000 hours assumes you bill every working hour of the year, that tax is not a thing, and that running a business is free. All three assumptions are wrong in the same direction, and together they usually underprice an independent worker by 40–60%.

The three deductions people forget

  • Tax and social contributions. As an employee your employer pays part of this for you. Once you invoice directly, the gross-to-net gap is yours: 25–45% depending on where you live and how you structure the work.
  • Business costs. Laptop, software, a share of rent and power, insurance, an accountant, payment fees, and the cost of the weeks you do not work. Ten to twenty percent of revenue is a realistic range for a solo consultant.
  • Non-billable time. Nobody pays for proposals, invoices, sales calls, bookkeeping, or the hour you spend learning the tool the client uses. Twenty-five billable hours in a 40-hour week is normal; forty is a business with no sales pipeline.

What the numbers look like

A $90,000 take-home target at a 25% tax rate needs $120,000 of gross profit. With business costs at 10% of revenue, you must invoice $133,333. At 25 billable hours across 48 weeks — 1,200 hours — the rate is $111 per hour. The same target priced at "$45 an hour" delivers roughly $36,000 of take-home, not $90,000. That gap is why experienced freelancers seem expensive on paper and still feel squeezed at the end of the year.

Quoting a project without regret

  1. Estimate the hours honestly, then add 20% for the revision rounds you always end up doing.
  2. Multiply by the hourly rate from this page. If the number feels too high for the client, the scope is too large — cut scope, not rate.
  3. Name the revision rounds (two is a fair default) and price extra rounds at the same hourly rate.
  4. Take a deposit — 30–50% is normal — and set the final payment before handover, not after.
  5. Write a kill fee: if the client cancels mid-project, you keep the deposit and bill the hours already worked.

When to charge by the day instead

Day rates work when the work is collaborative and hard to bound — workshops, audits, on-site consulting. A day rate of six to eight times the hourly rate is the usual convention, and this calculator shows both, so you can check that your day rate is not quietly becoming a discount for clients who book you in blocks.

The meeting policy that survives eight timezones

Timezone pain is rarely about arithmetic. Everyone can convert 15:00 Seoul to their own clock; the hard part is deciding whose evening pays for the meeting, every week, forever. Three rules keep a distributed team from quietly resenting the same three people.

Rotate the awkward hour

If Seoul and San Francisco need a weekly sync, someone is at 07:00 or 23:00. Alternate monthly. Written in the calendar as "odd months Seoul 07:00, even months San Francisco 08:00", the burden is visible and shared — which matters more than the hour itself.

Use "all at work" as the default, not the rule

The green rows on this page are hours when nobody is outside 09:00–18:00. Often there is exactly one, or none. When there is none, prefer moving the meeting to whoever's day it least disrupts and keeping it short, over finding a worse hour that technically fits everyone.

Watch the two weeks around a clock change

Most of the year, the Seoul–Berlin gap is eight hours; for the two weeks between Europe's last Sunday in March and the US second Sunday in March, it is seven or nine depending on which pair you are converting. Recurring meetings booked in January silently shift by an hour in March. Cells with a dotted border in the grid mark exactly those dates: a country's UTC offset changed during that hour.

Make the meeting worth the hour it costs

A meeting that pulls six people from four timezones costs six person-hours plus the context switching. Put the agenda and the pre-read in the invite, write down every decision in the shared document during the call, and move anything that is only a status update to asynchronous notes. The teams that handle timezones best meet less, and their calendars show it.

Quick conversions worth memorising

  • Seoul ↔ Tokyo: no gap. Same hour, no daylight saving in either country.
  • Seoul ↔ Singapore / Bali: one hour. Seoul is always ahead; neither Singapore nor Bali changes clocks.
  • Seoul ↔ London: eight hours most of the year, nine during the weeks when London is on summer time and Seoul is not (late March to late October).
  • Seoul ↔ San Francisco: 17 hours — which means a 09:00 meeting in Seoul is 16:00 the previous day in California.

How these numbers are built

Every calculator on this page runs entirely in your browser. No input is sent anywhere, nothing is stored on a server, and the same URL reproduces the same numbers — the parameters live in the address bar, so you can bookmark a scenario or send it to a colleague.

AI prices are the vendors' own published list prices, checked on 15 November 2026 and printed next to each model. Freelance rates use a straightforward gross-up: take-home ÷ (1 − tax) ÷ (1 − business costs), divided by billable hours. Timezone offsets follow each country's published rules, including US, EU, Australian and New Zealand daylight saving, and are applied per hour rather than per day so that the transition week is handled correctly.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate are the AI API prices?

They are list prices per million tokens in USD, checked on 15 November 2026 and shown beside each model. Prices move: Gemini 4 Argon rises to $4/$20 on 1 December 2026, Gemini 3.8 Flash to $1.50/$7.50 on 1 January 2027, and GPT-5 is deprecated on 11 December 2026. A "fast" or "priority" tier is usually double the standard rate, and batch or flex tiers are usually half.

Why is output so much more expensive than input?

Reading a prompt can be parallelised across a GPU batch; generating tokens happens one token at a time. Vendors pass that cost difference on, typically charging five to ten times more per output token. It is the single strongest reason to keep answers short.

Are reasoning tokens billed as output?

Yes on every provider listed here. Thinking tokens are generated tokens, so they are charged at the output rate even when the visible answer is short. A model with a low output price but long internal reasoning can therefore cost more than its headline rate suggests — which is why effort settings matter more than the model name.

Does prompt caching really cut 90% of the input bill?

Cached input reads are usually 10% of the fresh input price — 2.5% on some newer models — so yes, for the cached share. The write itself costs about 1.25× a normal input token, which is why caching a prefix you use once is a loss and caching one you use a thousand times is a large win.

Should I quote hourly or fixed price?

Fixed price when the scope is genuinely clear and you have done the work before; hourly when it is not. Use the hourly number from the second calculator as the floor for both, and add a revision buffer before you say a fixed number out loud.

What if there is no overlap at all?

Then the team is not really synchronous. Pick the pair that must meet live, rotate the inconvenient hour, and move everything else to written updates. The grid will show zero green rows — that is information, not a failure of the tool.

Do you store what I type?

No. The calculators are plain JavaScript in the page. Your inputs only exist in the browser and in the query string of the URL you are looking at. See the privacy page for the advertising cookies that third-party ad slots may set.
